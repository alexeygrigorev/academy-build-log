## A Closed Voucher Coaching School

Someone reached out after a Telegram post and mentioned an Instagram school that ran voucher-funded courses and then closed.

I checked the public record. This was not a Bildungsgutschein Weiterbildung course like ours. It was AVGS coaching (Aktivierungs- und Vermittlungsgutschein, § 45 SGB III). Nemusli GmbH in Bamberg presented itself as an AZAV-certified Träger in 2025 and offered online one-to-one Jobcoaching and Existenzgründungscoaching. The founder said she stopped working with the state in May 2026 and handed the licence back. She announced the closure on 29 June 2026. The public sources include the [company register](https://www.online-handelsregister.de/handelsregisterauszug/by/Bamberg/HRB/12020/Nemusli-GmbH), the [program page](https://nemusli-consult.com/jobcenter), the [accreditation posts](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vasilevavalentina_big-news-were-officially-accredited-activity-7368612602987397121-OlnN) and the [closure posts](https://t.me/nemusli/6398).

The founder gave three reasons. Invoices went unpaid because the GmbH was not exempt from VAT. Jobcenters preferred local offline providers, even though they cannot officially recommend a specific school. It was hard to find enough strong Russian- and Ukrainian-speaking coaches.

I compared treating this as a different product with no lesson for us, and treating it as a warning for our voucher work. The measure type is different, but the operations are close enough. I chose to keep the lessons. An AZAV certificate does not equal vouchers. An English online course will meet the same "we don't know you" and "we prefer a school around the corner" pattern. The VAT exemption has to be in place before the first cohort, not after the first invoice.

I added this case to the [AZAV plan]({{ '/research/azav-certification.html' | relative_url }}) and I will prepare a one-page brief for Berater. The full notes are in the [Nemusli research]({{ '/research/academy-competitor-nemusli.html' | relative_url }}).

## Staying on DataTalksClub

I considered a new school domain, the current subdomain, a path under [datatalks.club](https://datatalks.club/) and a short domain used only for redirects. DataTalksClub already has years of courses and inbound links. The domain dates from 19 September 2020.

The 1 October Moz crawl gave it Domain Authority 34, homepage Page Authority 45 and 1,140 referring domains, including 591 to the homepage. Two Berlin bootcamps had Domain Authority 32 and 25, with 918 and 611 referring domains. A larger international bootcamp stood far ahead at 59 and 5,114.

The main site had 1,548 sitemap URLs. The courses subdomain already appeared in search, with Page Authority 34 and 52 referring domains. The academy subdomain returned no results in the initial Google site search and had no backlinks.

The main homepage also lacked an academy link. I had 137 days until 1 April 2027. Starting a new domain meant building that presence from zero.

I chose the academy path under the existing domain. Google allows both subdomains and subfolders to rank; I made this choice for the existing links, brand and crawl history. The marketing recommendation was to wait until after the 7 December session to avoid changing event and newsletter links.

I chose to start the move now because changing those links is a small task. I asked for a preview, a homepage and navigation link, matching canonical tags and permanent redirects from every old URL. I kept a short redirect domain as a later option.

The move is planned. The new path still returns 404, and [academy.datatalks.club](https://academy.datatalks.club/) remains the live canonical host. [PR #19](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/academy/pull/19) added [Umami](https://umami.is/), a [sitemap](https://academy.datatalks.club/sitemap.xml) and [robots.txt](https://academy.datatalks.club/robots.txt). Both the sitemap and the robots file return 200. The robots file points to the sitemap. The sitemap lists the public academy pages: home, both course pages, sessions, privacy and impressum. The [domain research]({{ '/research/domain-and-seo-plan.html' | relative_url }}) compares the options.

## English Teaching and German Job Searches

Five people reached out after my outreach. The [test engineer's feedback](../2026-11-14/#what-i-learned-from-the-calls-and-from-reddit) shaped the interview practice. Here is what three more people told me.

A data scientist and former ML engineer in Berlin works part time at a startup and is job hunting. She thinks skills in LLMs, agents and RAG are in demand. The employment agency told her to improve her German rather than take extra qualification courses.

An academic ML researcher, reached through the same community, wants to move into industry and so far can't. She expected a gap in December and then another one-year academic contract from January. Her impression is that the agency isn't interested in helping. I decided that the program should teach people how to ask a counsellor for a training voucher, without promising a successful application.

A professional who just finished a notice period spent about one and a half months on benefits and starts a new job on 15 November. Cold applications converted poorly. Vendor certificates helped.

Almost all of the vacancies he saw require German, which he already has at C1. In his view the market wants data engineering plus AI plus German. He wants to learn solid practices for coding with AI tools.

After these conversations, I kept interview practice, counsellor preparation and a small German voucher page in the plan. Experienced developers remain the core audience, with test engineers and academic ML researchers as adjacent groups. The [interview summaries](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/interviews.md) cover the conversations.

I compared this feedback with the [Deutscher Startup Monitor 2025](https://startupverband.de/fileadmin/startupverband/mediaarchiv/research/dsm/Deutscher_Startup_Monitor_2025.pdf). It reports English as the working language at 67.1% of Berlin startups, against 37.3% nationally. International staff make up 42.3% in Berlin and 31.7% nationally. Berlin's English share was 55.8% in 2024.

The [IW study on AI specialists](https://www.iwkoeln.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Studien/Gutachten/PDF/2024/Die_Suche_nach_KI-Fachkr%C3%A4ften_in_Deutschland-2024.pdf) found that 31% of German AI-developer ads in 2019–2024 were in English, compared with 11% of comparable roles at the same employers and 4% of all ads. On [Job Drop Berlin](https://jobdropberlin.com/daily), 1,237 of 2,334 language-tagged startup roles, about 53%, were English-speaking on 15 November. These samples describe different parts of the market.

[Stepstone](https://www.thestepstonegroup.com/deutsch/newsroom/pressemitteilungen/ki-skills-werden-zum-karrierefaktor-nachfrage-hat-sich-seit-2019-verdreifacht/) found AI skills in 0.85% of ads in 2019, 2.62% in the first half of 2026 and 3.49% in June. In IT the share rose from 7.1% in 2025 to 12.2% in the first half of 2026. Generative AI rose from 3.6% to 17% of AI-relevant ads. I see a reason to teach current AI skills in English. I also see German as a real constraint for some jobs and funding decisions. The [German and skills research](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/german-vs-skills.md) collects the sources.

## Risks

### B2 German and Vouchers

One test engineer was told she gets a Bildungsgutschein only after passing B2 German. I chose to keep the program in English. I don't teach it in German. Without vouchers, the cohort doesn't launch.

Since 10 September 2026, the AZAV certifier guidance says participants in further training need sufficient German, "i.d.R. B2" (as a rule). That line is in [Umsetzungshinweis 2/2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/umsetzungshinweis-02-2026_ba059832.pdf). The Berater guidance for Weiterbildung has no fixed German threshold. There is an exception for learning German in parallel. Approval of an English course depends on proving that the field hires in English. A [June 2026 LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sergkuzn_an-interesting-dialog-happened-in-the-agentur-activity-7475868457406775296-W38s) describes a Munich Agentur case: the Berater agreed the English course fit the target roles, then still asked for a B2 certificate.

As a floor, about 41% of Berlin tech workers have B2+ German, from the [Handpicked Berlin 2025](https://handpickedberlin.com/salaries/2025/report/) survey, which is self-selected. Our estimate is about 75 to 200 voucher-eligible people in Berlin for 15 seats, counting the B2+ group.

I first target experienced developers with B2+ German who want an English AI course, online across Germany. Then I target people at B1 with a German plan. I don't promise a voucher to people at A2 or below. The details are in the [B2 German rule research]({{ '/research/b2-german-rule.html' | relative_url }}).

### AZAV Certificate Does Not Equal Vouchers

Nemusli GmbH presented itself as AZAV-certified in 2025, ran an online AVGS coaching program, and closed it in 2026. The founder still needed to visit Jobcenters in person. Berater steered people to local offline providers they already knew. I chose to treat this as a warning for our English online program: certification and a catalogue line are not enough. We need a one-page brief for Berater and a way to be a known option in the offices that issue vouchers. The details are in the [Nemusli research]({{ '/research/academy-competitor-nemusli.html' | relative_url }}).

### VAT Exemption

The same founder said invoices went unpaid because the GmbH was not exempt from VAT. I chose to put the § 4 Nr. 21 UStG certificate on the [AZAV plan]({{ '/research/azav-certification.html' | relative_url }}) before the first cohort, not after the first invoice. The [Nemusli research]({{ '/research/academy-competitor-nemusli.html' | relative_url }}) covers the case.

### Refusals for Experienced People

 Interviewees heard that they already had enough experience and didn't need training. I chose to include counsellor preparation in the free sessions and a one-page brief for Vermittler. [§ 81 Abs. 1a SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__81.html) allows training for unemployed people when new skills improve their job chances and fit the labour market. The [Berater objections guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/berater-objections.md) covers bringing five to ten current job ads and rejection letters, applying in writing and asking for a written Bescheid. For a refusal notified in Germany, the usual [Widerspruch deadline is one month](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgg/__84.html); the notice gives the route and deadline. I chose practical evidence over a promise of approval.

### AZAV Timing

 April leaves little room for delays. I need the provider certificate by January–February 2027, and course approval takes longer. I chose to start both approval tracks early, with a contract target around 30 November, rather than wait for the demand test to end. The [certification plan](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/azav-certification.md) sets out the dependencies.

### A Smaller Approved Markup

 The first cost case included a second instructor, expert teaching rates, agent tools and API costs. I changed the staffing plan to backup teachers and mentor reviews. I chose a separate reason and evidence for each real cost instead of assuming approval of the full markup. The certifier can still reduce it. The [cost case](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/azav-cost-justification.md) covers the alternatives.

### No Graduate Placement Data Yet

 The wider IT training results are weak. The BA's Förderung der beruflichen Weiterbildung report for July 2026 gives employment six months after training at 38.7% for IT, KldB 43, across Germany and 35.6%, about 36%, in Berlin. All courses nationally reached 48.2%.

These are sector figures, rather than results from my academy. I chose entry screening, employer projects, interview practice and a six-month follow-up for the first cohort. I also used the [job and bootcamp research](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/supply-jobs-and-bootcamps.md). The [market-sizing research and BA source](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log/blob/main/research/market-sizing.md) give the definitions.

### Tighter Berlin Funding

 Berlin IT training starts fell from 4,745 to 4,038, down 14.9%, in the 12 months to July 2026 while developer unemployment rose. Germany's starts rose about 3%. The Berlin Agentur's reduced IT training budget adds pressure. I chose Germany-wide outreach and clear evidence of the skills each applicant needs rather than relying on Berlin alone.

### Enough Eligible Learners

 Our estimate is about 75 to 200 voucher-eligible people in Berlin for 15 seats, and interest doesn't mean a voucher. I compared a Berlin-only cohort with wider online reach and chose Germany-wide recruitment. I use confirmed waitlists and the free sessions to learn how many people fit the experience, full-time and funding requirements. The course still needs enough voucher-eligible people for about 15 places.

## Why I Shared the Build

I chose to build the academy in public because the research also helps people considering voucher-funded courses. I compared an organisation repo with my personal GitHub, and a plain repo with a small website. I chose my personal [build-log repo](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/academy-build-log) and a site with dated pages. It went live at 08:20. Sharing the decisions also gives people a way to discover the academy before the first cohort.
