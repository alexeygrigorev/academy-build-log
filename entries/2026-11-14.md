## Our Audience

Today we researched the audience, job postings and existing courses. I want to teach experienced developers who were laid off, are in their notice period or are looking for work. We're starting with Berlin and making the sessions available online so people elsewhere can join.

We focused the offer on people who already write, change and test code at work. They need to add current AI skills to their experience, without spending months repeating programming basics. We also started asking developers about their experiences with training and looking for work, so we could check our assumptions before building the full program.

## Keeping Both Tracks

We're exploring [Agentic Engineering](https://academy.datatalks.club/agentic-engineering/), where developers use coding agents to build and ship software, while deciding what to build and checking the result. In [AI Engineering](https://academy.datatalks.club/ai-engineering/), developers build applications that use large language models, such as chatbots, document search and agents.

AI Engineering is easier to recognise in job titles, while we found fewer courses focused on coding agents for experienced developers. I lean towards Agentic Engineering, but we still need to compare interest from people who might actually join.

We worked on a roughly three-month course design. Five or six months felt too long for experienced developers trying to return to work. Each program will include an introduction to the other track, because building AI applications and using coding agents are useful together.

We also added interview preparation to both plans. We included mock interviews, coding exercises, take-home assignments, explaining projects and individual career consultations. We set aside the equivalent of two weeks within the course, with practice spread through the final phase. I want people to have time to practise the hiring process as well as learn technical skills.

## Three Free Sessions

We planned three free online sessions to explain the two paths, answer questions and learn what people need. During planning we called the series Hireable With AI, then chose the more direct wording "Between Jobs" for the audience and headline.

All three Meetup pages were published today.

The sessions are on Mondays at 17:00 Berlin time, on Zoom:

- [23 November: AI Engineering vs Agentic Engineering](https://www.meetup.com/berlin-datatalks-club/events/317427871/)
- [30 November: Agentic Engineering: How I Build Software with Coding Agents](https://www.meetup.com/berlin-datatalks-club/events/317427748/)
- [7 December: AI Engineering: How to Build Apps on Top of LLMs](https://www.meetup.com/berlin-datatalks-club/events/317427758/)

I chose a short talk of 15 to 20 minutes, followed by questions. We removed the earlier plans for live coding and hands-on demonstrations. Each description now says that the sessions are for developers with at least two years of experience who are looking for work. We also added a short point about getting a Bildungsgutschein for training.

We use Meetup for RSVPs and built a separate [sessions page](https://academy.datatalks.club/sessions/) with questions about people's background and interests. We want to learn which program they prefer. We asked for experiences in developer communities as well, rather than relying only on registrations.

## Making the Pages Clearer

The first landing pages felt too obviously generated. We rewrote the copy in plain English, removed vague phrases and unsupported statistics, and saved the approved wording for reuse across the site and events. We also made the course and event titles consistent.

By the evening, we had deployed the copy cleanup on the [academy home page](https://academy.datatalks.club/), both track pages and the sessions page. The public changes are in [PR #13: copy cleanup](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/academy/pull/13), [PR #15: consistent titles](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/academy/pull/15) and [PR #16: clearer course wording](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/academy/pull/16). I wanted visitors to understand what we'd teach and who it was for.

We asked for email confirmation across the academy forms and decided to count confirmed signups when comparing demand. That should help us separate real interest from test submissions and unconfirmed addresses. We also requested visits and track-choice counts, so we'd be able to compare signups with the traffic each page received.

Alongside the audience work, we prepared an AZAV certification plan and a draft quality handbook. April 2027 remained the target, with the certification work still ahead of us.
