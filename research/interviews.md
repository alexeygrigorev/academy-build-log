# Discovery Interviews (14 and 15 November 2026)

On 14 November 2026 I posted in a large data science and jobs Telegram community and asked people to talk to me. Five people reached out. Here is what four people told me.

 I do not promise job placement to anyone, and the course design uses "interview preparation" as the frame.

## 1. test engineer, Berlin (14 November)

**What she told me**

- Works as a test engineer, with a little programming (bots and test automation). She asked if she qualifies. I said yes for the course, but AI engineer jobs often require developer experience.
- About a year ago her employer closed its Berlin team and moved the work abroad.
- She registered with the Agentur and learned German from scratch. Her Berater said a Bildungsgutschein is possible, but only after she passes a B2 German exam.
- She is looking for a test engineer job, and it looks bad. Her former test engineer colleagues are still unemployed. Around 20 test engineer people she knows in Berlin have been searching for about a year. Even referrals do not always help. Openings get 200 to 300 applications, then close without a hire and open again.
- Interviews now often include algorithm tasks, even for manual test engineer roles.
- She is interested in the AI Engineering track (not Agentic).

**Later the same evening**

- In her experience, the Agentur gives vouchers very rarely.
- Her previous Berater refused a voucher at 4 or 5 appointments: "big experience, no need to study". She has about 10 years as a tester.
- She wrote letters saying the field is changing because of AI and interviews now need a programming language.
- Other reasons she heard: "in our Agentur we don't give vouchers", "you need 5 years of work in Germany" (she had that), and then "you did not pay enough taxes". We could not find any of these rules in the law.
- Her new Berater wants German first, then talks about training.
- People she knows who got a voucher were unhappy with the courses: huge classes, nobody checks the learning, people just sit out the hours.

**What I take from it**

- Interview preparation matters. Both programs get a dedicated interview block (mock interviews, take-homes, algorithm rounds, talking about projects). Not a job promise.
- test engineer / testers are an adjacent segment for the AI Engineering track.
- The main conversion risk is not "experienced people never go to the Agentur". It is the Berater refusing because of experience ("Sie sind qualifiziert genug"), plus local differences and rules that are not in the law. See [Berater objections](berater-objections.md).
- We tell people upfront: a voucher is not guaranteed. Since 1 January 2025 the Agentur also decides on training for Jobcenter clients, so the Jobcenter is not a second place to get the same voucher.
- Course quality is a second issue: people with a voucher can still refuse a weak, unsupervised class.

## 2. Data scientist / former ML engineer, Berlin (15 November)

Source: a short written Q&A.

- Data scientist and former ML engineer. Works part-time at a startup and is job hunting.
- Thinks skills in LLMs, agents and RAG are in demand.
- The Agentur told her to improve her German rather than take extra qualification courses.

**What I take from it**

- Same as the test engineer case: German first, training later. It is a live example of the "German first" objection, not proof that a voucher is legally blocked until a language exam. See [German versus skills](german-vs-skills.md).
- The useful public line is not "you don't need German in Germany". It is: in Berlin tech many roles hire in English, AI skills show up in job ads, and you can ask a Berater who sends you to a German course to look at those ads and put a refusal in writing.

## 3. ML researcher moving from academia to industry (15 November)

Source: a short note after she reached out through the same community.

- Researcher in machine learning, data science, modelling and optimization.
- Wants to move to industry and so far cannot. Moves between short academic contracts.
- Her impression of the Agentur: they are not interested in helping.

**What I take from it**

- Possible adjacent segment: people with research experience in ML who want industry roles. Next to the core persona, not instead of it.
- Many people write the same about the Agentur. So we should teach people how to ask a Berater for a Bildungsgutschein. That belongs in the sessions and in a participant guide, not as a promise that a better argument gets the voucher approved.

## 4. Data professional after a notice period (15 November)

Source: a chat.

- Finished his notice period, spent about one and a half months on benefits, and starts a new job on 15 November.
- Cold applications worked poorly. Databricks certificates helped.
- Almost all openings he saw require German. He has C1.
- In his view the market wants data engineering plus AI plus German.
- He wants to learn good practices for coding with AI tools.

## What we decided after these talks

- Both programs include an interview preparation block: one full week plus more practice in the last weeks next to the capstone, plus individual career talks and a written CV review. This is interview preparation, not a job placement promise.
- We tell people early that a voucher is not guaranteed and that experienced applicants are often refused. The free sessions include a short item on how to get a Bildungsgutschein. We also teach how to prepare for the talk with the Berater.
- Public German content is one page only (`/academy/bildungsgutschein/`). Everything else is in English.
- The core audience stays experienced developers. test engineer / testers and people moving from academic ML are adjacent segments.

Still open:

- The exact public wording about vouchers and about German versus AI skills.
- Whether the German Bildungsgutschein page is the next page to write.
- How to handle BA guidance that training usually expects B2 German. For now this is a documented risk, not a product decision.

## What Reddit says

See [voucher course complaints](voucher-course-complaints.md) and [laid-off developers and the Agentur](laid-off-developers-and-agentur.md). In short: experienced people in Germany often describe voucher courses as built for career changers and beginners, too basic, a waste of time and of unclear help for getting hired. Berater also refuse experienced people because they are "already qualified". Long job searches continue even after an ML or AI bootcamp.
